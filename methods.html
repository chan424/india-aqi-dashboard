AQI formula

For each pollutant the sub-index is

I = (Ihi − Ilo) / (BPhi − BPlo) × (C − BPlo) + Ilo

Concentration is truncated (micrograms to an integer, CO to one decimal) before the lookup, which is what makes CPCB’s own examples land on integers: PM2.5 of 31 µg/m³ → 51, 45 → 75, 60 → 100. The AQI is the worst sub-index. CPCB publishes an overall AQI when at least three pollutants are present and one of them is PM2.5 or PM10. This site still shows a partial index if that bar is missed, and says so.

Severe has no published upper concentration. The upper end used here is the top of Very Poor plus the width of Very Poor, so the index reaches 500 at a finite value (PM2.5 at 380 µg/m³, PM10 at 510). Above that, the index stays at 500.

The estimate

  1. Take the last 24 hourly CAMS values (at least 16 hours). PM2.5, PM10, NO2 and SO2 use that mean. Ozone and CO use the highest 8-hour mean inside those 24 hours. CO is converted from µg/m³ to mg/m³ by dividing by 1000.
  2. Apply a straight-line correction, concentration = a + b × CAMS, fitted on city-day means for . Training days are 2022–2024. A day counts only when the station file has at least 18 hours. The line is kept only if it lowers 2025 bulletin RMSE.
  3. Turn the corrected concentrations into sub-indices and take the worst.
  4. Inverse-distance residuals from neighbouring cities were tested and are not applied. See the table.
  5. Where the CPCB bulletin names the same city, the card also shows that value as Measured (monitor). It is a city figure. The station-level CPCB feed is requested by the hourly job; when it does not answer, the map says so and keeps the city bulletin.

Holdout against the CPCB city bulletin

The historical score uses daily CAMS means, including for ozone and CO. The live estimate uses 8-hour maxima for those two. Bias is prediction minus bulletin. Category rates are percent of days.

Limitations

  • The calibration is four cities: Hyderabad, Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada and Chittoor. Using it in Delhi or a Telangana district with no monitor is a transfer, and the ± band is the Andhra–Hyderabad holdout error, not a local interval.
  • On the 2025 bulletin the calibration lowers RMSE a little (about 41 to 39) and raises the low bias (about −6 to −16). Raw CAMS ozone is much too high and can inflate the raw AQI by accident. After the ozone correction the index sits further below the bulletin in 2025. In the 2026 months available in the archive the calibrated bias is near zero and RMSE is lower. 2026 was not used to choose the model.
  • IDW helped on only about half of city-days, because the CAMS cities are far apart, and it made RMSE worse. It is switched off.
  • The CPCB PDF lists four Andhra Pradesh stations. The 21 November 2026 bulletin reports monitors in many more AP cities, including Ongole. Those cities are drawn as “coordinates unpublished”, not as invented pins.
  • District gaps use Census 2011 polygons. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have been split into smaller districts since then. A parent polygon can hide a gap inside a new district, or the reverse.
  • City-bulletin colour is painted on every station in that city. It is not that station’s own analyser.
  • Open-Meteo can fail. The hourly GitHub Action writes data/cache/city_estimates.json so the cards still load. A click on the map needs the live API.
  • Nothing here is a health warning or a substitute for the CPCB bulletin.
